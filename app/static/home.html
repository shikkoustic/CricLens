How it works

One clip, four steps, under two minutes.

  1. 1

    Find the batter

    A trained detector picks the striker out of the frame — not the bowler, keeper or a fielder — even in a crowded broadcast shot.

  2. 2

    Track the pose

    Body joints are tracked frame by frame through the backswing, contact and follow-through, anchored on the moment of bat-ball contact.

  3. 3

    Classify & score

    A sequence model names the shot; a second model rates technique across five body parts plus an overall score.

  4. 4

    Get feedback

    Stride length and hand speed are measured where the footage allows, and the findings are written up as plain-language coaching notes.

What you get

A full read on one delivery.

Shot classification

Identifies the stroke — drive, cut, pull/hook, sweep and more — with a confidence score, not just a guess.

Technique scoring

An overall rating plus a per-body-part breakdown (head, shoulders, hands, hips, feet), benchmarked against a coached reference set.

Movement metrics

Stride length and peak hand speed, measured directly from the footage when the crease or stumps are visible enough to calibrate.

Coaching feedback

The measured findings are turned into a short, plain-language write-up — what worked, what to work on, and why.

Built to be honest about its limits

We'd rather tell you what the numbers can and can't say.

The five body-part scores are trained on a coached reference dataset where they move together more than five truly independent ratings should — so treat the overall score as the main signal and the per-part breakdown as a secondary read, not five separate verdicts. Every clip shown here is held out from the data the models were trained on, so you're seeing how they generalise, not memorised answers.
